**One golfer-model change to know about.** Under the real physics, good players never aced most holes, because the AI only plays safe planned routes. From Round 3 on, good golfers go for the hole's best-found ace line on 30% of tee shots. Each attempt carries ±1.5° aim and ±8% power error on top of normal stroke noise, to model hitting a remembered line. I set this once and didn't tune it per hole. The Round 3 and 4 ace rates depend on it; with it switched off, those rates would be much lower.

**Changes per round** (the earlier old-physics rounds r1–r4 are summarised briefly in `playtest-log.md`):
- **Round 1** (old geometry, real physics): no ace for good players on six holes, and no ace line at all on H3 and H6. H6 was too hard (4.70). H8's ring was a free birdie (3.13). Average players aced H9 9.3% of the time through the secret knot.
- **Round 2:** compacted H3 and H6, lined the H5 tee up with the root bridge, added a sliding door in front of the H8 ring, and tightened the H9 knot. The sim now treats a ball resting against a rail on a slope as stopped (this fixed the H7 timeouts). H3 then became too easy and H2 aced too often.
- **Round 3:** added the ace-attempt model and moved cups and a bumper stool. This exposed ace lines a good player could repeat: H1 21%, H2 33%, H9 23%.
- **Round 4:**
  - H1's cup moved off the deflector's exit line.
  - H2 was lengthened by about 4 studs.
  - H6's cup was nudged.
  - H8's ring was shrunk.
  - H9's knot moved out of the rail corner onto open floor, with its exit aimed at the cup.

**Remaining warning:** one timeout in 2000 average-player plays on H7, most likely the sliding log pinning a ball against a rail.
